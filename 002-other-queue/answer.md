# Is the other queue really faster?

Usually, yes. With three equal lines, some other line beats yours two times in three. In 1,000,000 simulated trips it was 667,234 times, and the exact answer is 2/3. No single line is faster than yours: any one named line beat you 500,830 times, a coin toss. There are just two of them to lose to.

In a simulated shop with three tills at 90% busy, looking first and joining the shortest line cut the average wait by about two thirds, from 17.7 minutes to 6.0. One shared line gave 5.5 minutes, about 9% better than the shortest line. Its main gain was order: 402,161 of 1,000,000 customers were overtaken under the shortest-line rule, and none under a shared line.

These are simulations with assumed numbers, not measurements of a real shop. Checkouts are random with a two-minute average and nobody leaves the queue. Code and data: this folder.
